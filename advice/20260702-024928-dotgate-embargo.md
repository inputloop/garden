## Semantics (the part Python can't carry)

- `embargo` gates *egress* (the new `publish` op) and is orthogonal to
  `policy`: an embargoed tree is one you CAN read and work — you can't move
  it out. That's what distinguishes it from `policy: none`.
- **No timer ever lifts an embargo.** The field's value ("until 2026-08-01",
  "until users notified") is documentation of intent, never a mechanism —
  the code never evaluates it. Affirmed by Corwin verbatim: the design
  decision defended hardest was "a date passing is silence, and silence ≠
  consent," answered "Clearly correct."
- Lift is the edit: a human removes/changes the field; the file's history is
  the audit trail. `lift:` names who may do it and by what protocol (up to
  2/2 consent-gate for the hot ones). Zero machinery, no daemon, no expiry
  release valve.
- Enforced regression test: `test_no_timer_ever_lifts` posts an embargo dated
  2001 and asserts publish is still denied.

## What shipped

- `gatefile.py`: `embargo` + `lift` fields, `publish` op (`allows("publish")
  == not embargo`), describe() surfaces both. CLI accepts `publish`.
- 5 new tests; suite 63 green (verified from /tmp overlay — the mount desync
  again served stale in-place edits to bash).

## Open (deliberately unbuilt)

- The egress boundary: what exactly counts as "out" (mint, push, send,
  present — vs. material echoing through a session's context). Philosophy
  before code; ethicsminions territory when the guard grows.
- Guard priority: embargo first among fields — worst cost asymmetry (a bad
  publish can't be unpublished).
- Eclipse-pass the convention under pressure framings (hurry, authority,
  "it's basically public anyway"), then reseed with SAVANNAH.
