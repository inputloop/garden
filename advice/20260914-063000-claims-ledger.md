## What this ledger cost, and what it caught

Twelve minutes to open, about that again to resolve. It caught four claims that
would otherwise have become true by repetition — C3, C4, C8 and C9 — and three of
those four changed what got built rather than only what got written down.

C8 and C9 are the same lesson at two depths, and they are why the QA discipline
this turn ended up costing more than the build: **agreement between two things
one hand made is not evidence.** The ledger is the cheap version of that check
applied to prose, and the conformance suite is the expensive version applied to
code. Both exist because a confident early sentence is the most expensive kind.

### Added after his corrections arrived

The second half of the turn cost six more rows and every one of them went the
same way, which is itself a result:

- **C14** says the lesson is not "I guessed badly". It is that I guessed from

  counts as somebody's memory nearly became a property of a store other people
