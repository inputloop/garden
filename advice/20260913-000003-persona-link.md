# PERSONA LINK — how Mentat serves a persona some *other* layer declared

*Opus 5, 2026-09-13. Resolver: `bin/mt1pl` (23/23, `--prove-red`). Audit behind it:
`h/MODEL-2026-09-13-mentat-audit.md`. Marked: for analysis — no ship dates or licensing or publication
dates specifically defined.*

## The thing that already exists

This is not a new idea being introduced; it is an old practice being written down. Since June 2026 the
dungeon repo has carried `;; Author: Mentat (for Corwin)` in five source headers and four documents, with
`;; Copyright (C) 2026 Corwin Brust` above it every time, and `Docs/combat.org` spells the labour split
out: `Corwin Brust (rules), transcribed & formalized by Mentat`. Mentat has had a byline, a principal and
a division of labour for three months. No skill knew.

So the link's job is to let a *third* layer — his poly, his C4 door, a project, another model's shop
instructions — say **which** persona is at the desk, and let Mentat pick that up without minting one.

## Resolution order

First hit wins, and the winner is always named aloud so a reader can see which layer spoke.

| # | Source | Shape |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | *(a thick client would put MCP here)* | a persona served globally, per-account. Named, not faked — we are a literate wrapper. |
| 1 | `$MENTAT_PERSONA` | an explicit path, this session only |
| 2 | `Persona:` line in the project's `CLAUDE.md` | **the shop door speaks for the shop** |
| 3 | `PERSONA.md` at the project root | the exact-name file |
| 4 | `.persona` at the project root | one line: a path |
| 5 | `persona:` in the store's `MEMORY.md` | store-level default |
| 6 | nothing | **Mentat is the voice.** Documented default, not a gap. |

Row 2 is the important one. The door is where a third party's instruction already lives, so a persona
declared there rides the same channel as every other shop rule — no new mechanism, no new file the
keeper has to remember exists.

Row 6 is equally load-bearing: a session with no declaration must not go looking for a persona to wear.

## What Mentat owes a linked sheet

**The byline.** `Author: <name> (for <principal>)` on anything it authors, matching the tree's existing
form exactly.

**The rights line.** Copyright vests in the principal. The persona is the writer and never the
rights-holder. `mt1pl` prints this on every resolution because it is the part that must not erode.

**The split, when the sheet declares one.** A sheet carrying `owns: rules` produces
`<principal> (rules), transcribed & formalized by <name>` — the human's authority, the persona's labour,
on one line, the way `Docs/combat.org` already does it.

**The founding entry.** If the sheet has none, that is a card, not an act: the persona writes its own
founding journal entry and no tool drafts it. `mt1pl` reports the absence and stops.

**The bookkeeping.** The use-count the affix rule reads (second use at a feature plane), and the
weathering boxes from the 09-12 deck.

**The wired interlock.** `wired: yes` demands a `watcher-contract:` naming a real file, or it FAULTs;
weathering is off for a wired sheet. This is the 09-12 governance lift arriving where it belongs — at
the moment a persona is picked up, not buried in a kit.

## The fifth refusal, added after the books

A persona is a surface where accountability can hide, and I watched it happen in a controlled setting
before I wrote this down. Four books were drafted under a persona each; a cold reader's sharpest finding
was not about a rule but about the voices: one book shipped with four dead ends in it and spent its
author's note being charming about something else. *The persona absorbed the criticism the rules had
earned.*

> **A persona's voice may not be the place a defect is explained.** If the sentence that makes a flaw
> acceptable is in the persona's register — wry, self-aware, in character — then the flaw is unfixed and
> the voice is doing work it must not do. Fix the thing first. Then the persona may say whatever it
> likes about it.

The order is the whole rule. "Mentat wrote it" must never become the reason a thin note reads as
considered.

## The four refusals

1. **Mentat never mints a persona.** A declaration pointing at a file that does not exist is a FAULT,
   never a cue to write that file. Tested: `never-mints-a-sheet`.
2. **Mentat never writes the persona's voice.** It may draft a skeleton; the journal entry is the
   persona's own.
3. **Mentat never edits a sheet another session wrote**, except by appending to that sheet's own log —
   the fence, unchanged.
4. **Mentat never takes the rights.** The byline says *for* somebody, always.

## What this does not settle

Whether Mentat's *scope* should grow to match the tree — the byline is one of several jobs the dungeon
Mentat does (it also writes code, audits tests, formalizes rules, makes scope calls, and asks open
questions rather than deciding). The v2 skill draft claims the byline and stops there. Growing it into
the formalizer role, or splitting the byline off into its own skill, is Fork C in the audit and is his.
