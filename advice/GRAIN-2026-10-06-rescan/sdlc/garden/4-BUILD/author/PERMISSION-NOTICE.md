# Permission notice for fan works

**DRAFT. Prepared by the technology team for the author and his counsel. It is not in force, it
grants nothing, and it is not legal advice. Until the author signs a final version, nothing on this
stick lets anyone publish anything.**

STATUS: DRAFT
VERSION: 0.1
AUTHOR: [the author's name, as he wants it printed]
SIGNED: no

## What this notice would say, once signed (in plain words)

1. **You may** write new stories set in the world of the titles in Schedule A and share them [CHOOSE
   ONE: with other owners of this stick | with the public (only after the author's separate signed
   contract)], free of charge, with the mark below on every copy.
2. **You may not** sell them or use them for any commercial purpose; copy more than [N] words of the
   author's text; use his name or cover art as if the work were his; or touch anything Schedule A
   leaves out (translations, audio, film, games: [AUTHOR TO CHOOSE]).
3. **The mark.** Every copy carries the text under "The mark" below.
4. **The author does not read fan works.** He does not read, review, approve or endorse them and owes
   nothing for them. You agree he may write anything, including something like your story, without
   owing you. Please do not send fan works to him. [COUNSEL TO CONFIRM]
5. **Taking it back.** The author may withdraw this permission for future works by a dated notice at
   [WHERE]. Works already shared stay as they are unless they break these terms. [COUNSEL TO CONFIRM]
6. **Works made with AI help.** [CHOOSE ONE: allowed, if the work says so | allowed, if none of the
   author's text was given to the AI | not allowed]. This stick can start with or without AI; this
   is where the author says which applies.
7. **Only what the author owns is covered.** Schedule A lists titles and rights the author has checked
   he still holds. A right he sold, for example audio, is not covered whatever this notice says.

## The mark (the stamp tool prints exactly this, with the braces filled in)

<!-- watermark -->
Published by permission of {AUTHOR} under permission notice v{VERSION}.
{AUTHOR} has not read, reviewed or endorsed this work.
<!-- /watermark -->

The second line is the point. "By permission" alone could be taken to mean the author approved the
story; he did not read it. The stamp tool will not print a mark unless this notice is marked in
force AND the author's own signature on it verifies.

## Questions for the author and counsel (not part of the notice)

- Is a standing permission, given once for a class of works, the "written OK" the author wants
  before anything derived from his books is published? Or does he want to see each one?
- Who is covered: any owner of a stick, or only people he names? How far may a work travel?
- How is the permission withdrawn, and what happens to works already out?
- Do publishers or other contracts limit what he may allow? (He holds the rights his publishers do
  not; each title and each right still has to be checked.)
- What is his position on AI-assisted writing, and on his books being given to an AI at all?
- Voice: this draft says "the author" on purpose. He or counsel may prefer "I".

The SPDX line at the top covers this drafting only. It grants nothing in any work of the author.
