**Where all four agree.** The push is the right place for a control, and refusing a ban was right: A from the disuse literature, B from release practice, C because memory is context and not enforcement, D by explicit concession. The gate was fitted to its one incident and one vendor. Defaults upstream (the vendor's attribution setting, the app's output folder) should be fixed before the gate has to catch them. An undeclared published project gets advice, not a stop, and every paper names that as the soft spot. And each paper's Limitations says its author is the model it studies.

That last agreement matters most and is the least comfortable. Four papers agreeing is weaker evidence than four papers, because one model under one brief produced the agreement. *Falsifier:* a second model family reading the same pack (S21) reaches the same verdicts on the first four points.

**Where they truly disagree: what decides how hard a push is stopped.** They split on the variable, not on the facts. A keeps the audience as the variable and attacks the default: an undeclared remote should be an asked question, because people put declarations off. D changes the variable: a local path, a misnamed file or a vendor link harms whether or not anyone installs, since a public history is cloned, mirrored and scraped regardless. B states the field's rule (public is public) and declines to override his. C reaches D's side through his own kid-glove rule (a gate where the act cannot be undone) and stops there. The reason is that each protects a different party. A protects people's expectations, and expectations follow audience; D protects the commons and third parties, who are not an audience; B protects the artifact; C guards against a rule that memory fails to fire.

This is S08, his call. My verdict: do not decide it on argument. A's advice-rate audit and B's false-positive census are one replay. Run once over his published histories, it prices D's change in his own numbers (N-EDITOR-1). If the privacy checks rarely misfire, D's change costs little and the argument ends.

Two smaller disagreements are real but narrow. On copyright, B says keep the notices and ask the licensing experts first; D says scope the lines now. Neither drops the licences; they differ on order, and D's line census of card-games settles the order faster than a letter. On the human reader, D recommends the gate plus a human who reads and pushes; A's complacency findings say a human reading after "0 flags" is weaker than assumed. They fit together if the human reads before the gate reports. One operator's note: fork B as written falls between two of the gate's levels. `users` lets an agent push clean commits; `hand` stops every hooked push, the keeper's included. Choosing B means choosing which level carries it.

**What each lens sees alone.**
- **A** sees the keeper as the system's weakest part: advice repeated is advice ignored, a clean result is read as wider than it is, coded names load any second user, and the record that supervises the instrument is written by it. It also sees that "users" means two audiences: installers, who get MELPA's file list, and readers of the history, who get everything. Its active-choice recommendation (ask once at a release tag or package recipe) survives the errata and is in no step.
- **B** alone ran the code and found the defect of the night: the gate passed its own self-test while letting the whole slip through in three situations. That became D1. B alone sees the licence layer (14 of 26 files state two licences), MELPA's header form (a persona name is not an agent name), and that Forgejo ships server hooks off for a reason.
- **C** alone gives the house a reusable sorting rule: boundary rules belong in gates, judgment rules in memory. It alone reads AIMED as a security property (an instruction file in a package is an injection channel for every user's agent), finds the light shelf's experiment B confounded by a known law of word change, and finds the token-optimizer's replication unwitnessed in what it fetched.
- **D** alone works at scales beyond the shop: law, the commons, the vendor's terms, the grid. It alone sees that the house gates what is written down and not what is sent, that the "ethics redundancy" fails together with the keeper, and that his lift test can be turned on his own gates.

**What the burn-down changes.** It closes more than it opens. The gate fails closed, covers other vendors, finds local paths and says what it did not check; card-games is declared `users`; the trailer is out of the house form. A's §3.4 objections, most of B's §3.3, C's "fits one incident" and D's "fitted to one vendor" are answered in code. Three things survive: the variable (S08); bypass, since a client hook can be skipped; and self-grading. The repairs were written by the instrument that wrote the gate, so D's "grades its own homework" now applies to them. His own standing rule answers it: what got past the tests gets a new spec in a separate language (N-EDITOR-2). The burn-down also changes method: a planted fault is how you know a reader works, and two small readers with different windows beat one large one. That is the cheap answer to "one hand".

**Cut and keep.** One cut applies to the whole set: a 182-word paragraph on the gate's new shape is byte-identical in all four errata. Print it once, in a set preface, and point to it. The cuts below are verdicts; what each next edition says remains its author's.
- **A.** Cut: fold §3.4's three pushbacks and §4.5's wins into one "closed since" note; shrink §3.6 to the persona guard and E5, since its key effect did not replicate, as A says. Keep: §3.5 entire; the two audiences; the active choice; the keeper's cold read; E4 and E6.
- **B** needs the least. Cut: move the probe table and the diff to an appendix and cite 4af7de1; they are history. Keep: the licence counts, the MELPA header point, "the sane thing" on history, Experiment 2, and its own that licence lines on drawer files may be ceremony.
- **C.** Cut: the qualia from the ranked recommendations (the naming is Corwin's, as C says); the claim that a draw of four skills shows the garden sorting itself, since four cannot show a sort; and the Methods word counts, which drive nothing. Keep: §3.2 entire; AIMED as injection channel; experiment B's confound and its randomized repair; the thrash case at 17; the token-optimizer finding, with its present care.
- **D.** Cut: "passed through about 70 skills and 242 gate words". Words are not gates, and none was meant to fire at a push; its firing census makes the point honestly. Keep: the concessions; the vendor as a place data goes; common mode; copyright scoped, not dropped; the fork. D cites my own sheet among its persona cues. I leave that line alone; I am not a neutral reader of it.

## 5. Editor's note on the authorial voices

Verdict: one voice contributed material worth noting, D's. The set as a whole is one hand, and nothing further should be documented.

The self-descriptions differ more than the prose does. Three of four call themselves "dry". All four keep observed apart from judged, tag concerns and end objections with falsifiers, because the brief and the house require it. A describes "dense semicolon-chained sentences", yet by count it has 9 semicolons per thousand words, against B's 19 and C's 18. C says it is close to the house voice, and it is.

D did something the others did not. It writes its concessions before its objections, and it is the only paper in the first person singular: 14 uses of I, my or me, against 3 to 5 in the others. That order let its hardest points (common mode, harm before audience, the human reader) reach Corwin as forks rather than standing cautions.

B's largest contribution, the fail-open defect, came from running the code, not from its voice. Its "the sane thing" is git's phrase, used well.

Rule 8 asks nothing more.
