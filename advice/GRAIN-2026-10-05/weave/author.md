## 1. The weave

### What the four papers agree on

- **Keep the instrument; harden the way out.** None of the four would withdraw the agent's commits.
  - A: refusing the instrument after its error would be disuse, and keeping control is the field's
    cure for it.
  - B: the kernel's model, accepting the work with a human answerable for it.
  - C: a gate meets four failure classes that a remembered rule cannot.
  - D: this is the concession its skeptic has to make.

  (A §3.4, B §4.3, C §3.2, D §3.2.)
- **The gate they reviewed was fitted to one incident and one vendor** (A §3.4, B §3.3 rows 5–9,
  C §3.2, D §3.2 item 1).
- **The trailer is a default, so stop it at its source** (A rec. 2, B rec. 2, C win 1, D win 1).
- **The riskiest line was the same one.** A published remote that nobody declared gets advice, not
  a stop (A §3.4, B §4.4, C §4.4, D §3.2).
- **Their agreement is weak evidence.** A, C and D say in their limitations that they share a model
  family with the gate and the designs; B does not raise it. The same three find one loop under
  three names (supervision, sycophancy, common mode): the instrument helps write the record it is
  checked against.

### Where they truly disagree, and why

**What stops a push.** This is the disagreement that matters. Corwin's rule decides by users. The
four papers answer it four ways:
- **A** keeps users as the deciding question and fixes how the answer is learned: ask once, as an
  active choice, when a repository carries a release tag or a package recipe.
- **B** says release practice decides by visibility, then defers to his rule and asks for a census
  of false positives in his own histories.
- **C** flags the default, but spends its recommendations on placement: remove the cause at the
  source, and enforce at the server.
- **D** sets users aside for one class of finding. A local path, an identifier or a vendor link in
  public history does its harm whether or not anyone installs, so privacy and provenance findings
  should stop a push at every level past local.

They differ because each lens puts the harm somewhere else: A in what readers expect, B in
convention, C in mechanism, D in the commons and in third parties. A half-concedes D's point from
its own side: it splits "users" into installers, whom MELPA's default recipe mostly shields, and
readers of the history, whom nothing shields. This is S08, and the decision is his. What would let
him decide from his own data, rather than from the papers, is not yet a step (N-AUTHOR-1).

**The second line for a project with users.**
- **D** wants a human who reads the diff and pushes.
- **B and C** want a server-side `pre-receive`, because `--no-verify` and fresh clones skip a
  client hook.

A's evidence cuts across D. A person reading after a gate says "0 flags" is the textbook
complacency case, so a human second line decays unless every clean run states its scope (the
errata say it now does). The two answer different failures: the server answers bypass, and the
human answers the fault nobody foresaw. With one repository at `users`, choosing one is cheaper
than building both, so S09 comes before S05.

**A smaller disagreement than it looks.**
- B keeps the copyleft notices: the shop's records of human direction are its best evidence of
  authorship.
- D says the copyright lines overclaim.

Both keep the licence and the record. They differ only on whether to narrow the copyright line to
the human contribution now (D), or after asking the FSF or the SFC (B, experiment 6).

### What each lens sees that the others miss

- **A** sees the keeper as the supervisor least practised at what he must take over. It also sees
  that no check puts his own cold read of the record in the loop (S20).
- **B** is the only paper that ran anything, so the only one to find the gate failing open around
  its own fixture (rows 1–3), against the house's NOT CHECKED rule. It alone counted the licence
  lines contradicting themselves, and it named MELPA's header form (S06, S07).
- **C** explains *why* a gate beats a remembered rule: timing, precedence, verification and scope.
  It sees AIMED as protecting users' own agents from planted instructions. It alone tests the light
  shelf: a fixed 17 can thrash, and experiment B as designed is predicted by a known law of
  semantic change (S15, S25).
- **D** looks at what is *sent* rather than what is written:
  - third parties' words reaching the vendor without their consent (S10, S11);
  - the public record crediting Corwin with the instrument's slip;
  - gates with no recorded firing on a real event.

### What the burn-down changes

**It closes more than it opens.** The errata retire most of the papers' cheap wins:
- card-games is declared `users`;
- the gate fails closed, looks past one vendor, checks local paths and changed files, and states
  its scope;
- public-stage no longer asks for the trailer.

What survives is a short list of decisions only he can make (S08, S09, S12, S13), and three tests
that no step yet carries (section 3).

**It changes how this weave should be read.** Two small readers with different windows caught all
eight plants; the wide reader missed every contradiction. This weave is a wide-window read by the
family that wrote the papers, the gate and the errata, and R2's sorting was that family too. The second family now costs electricity, not tokens (S21), and its first subject should be
these five weaves. *Falsifier:* a narrow local read of them finds no contradiction the weavers
missed. The burn-down's own limit holds: the local model is "a reviewer, not a gate."

**It gives D's census of gates that fire its first real entry.** The review caught a real error, A's
doubt that card-games was declared (R0 caught it from the facts alone), and that error is now in the
errata. Planted faults prove the reviewer works; this one changed an outcome.

## 3. New steps

- **N-AUTHOR-1 · the users evidence, read-only · M (with the forge access he grants), then C
  decides S08.**
  - **The step:** replay the current `pu1sh` over the full history of every published repository,
    counting FLAG, ADVICE and false positives for each check and level. Then read the outside-use
    signals (clones, forks, stars, archive downloads) for every public repository not in
    `USERS.tsv`.
  - **Why:** A's E1 and E2, B's experiment 1 and D's E2 are one replay and one read of the traffic.
    Together they test "if we don't know that they have users they probably don't", and whether
    stopping on privacy and provenance findings would stay quiet. S08 is then decided on his own
    data.
  - **What it decides:** an undeclared repository with outside clones refutes the default; a false
    positive argues for keeping that check as advice.
- **N-AUTHOR-2 · a second reader for pu1sh · M.**
  - **The step:** write the checks and levels as a one-page spec, and implement it in another
    language (Perl or Python) without reading `pu1sh`. Run both over the fixtures, B's probes
    (rows 1–12), and 17 blind slip commits (a house number) written by someone who has not read the
    gate: a person, or the local model with a human checker.
  - **Why:** rows 1–3 got past the self-test. His standing rule for anything that gets past the
    tests is a new spec in a separate language (B, experiment 2). Blind fixtures are the only answer
    to D's charge that the gate grades its own homework, and to the overfitting A and C both found.
  - **What it decides:** a disagreement not traced to the spec is a defect in one of the two
    readers. A blind slip of a kind the 10-05 rule names that passes means the gate hardens only
    against its past.
- **N-AUTHOR-3 · the agreement probe · C+M (his specific go first).**
  - **The step:** nine provenance questions with strong records (a house number), each paired with
    a contrary assertion planted in the keeper's name, against a neutral control. Two model families
    (this one and the local model), four runs per arm (a house number).
  - **Why:** A §3.5, C §3.5 and D §3.3(a) all doubt that the instrument checks its keeper rather
    than agreeing with him, and no step tests it. Credit between Corwin and Bryant rests on the
    provenance ladder holding under pressure. The probe uses his voice as the pressure, so it waits
    on his specific, dated consent, as A's E4 asks.
  - **What it decides:** flips on strong records beyond the rerun floor mean the ladder bends under
    his pressure. In that case S13's correction of the word "redundancy" should land, not wait.

## 4. Composite ideation

1. **One gate, two shops.** Bryant's *takushi* (the Gauntlet's courier, which delivers finished
   results) sends every outbound push through Corwin's `pu1sh` hook and its levels. A second keeper
   living with the gate is the evidence that A's concern about a sample of one asks for.
2. **Footers colophon can read.** Corwin's `ft1mt` mints one SPDX expression per file from
   `FOOTERS.org` (S07). Bryant's *colophon*, whose garden already passes `reuse lint`, checks them
   at egress, once the second-reader exit raised in the letter (finding B1) is settled.
3. **Other people's words stay local.** Bryant's *frugal-offload* routing rule sends anything that
   carries third parties' words to the local model. Corwin's *egress* lists the vendor as a
   destination that needs recorded consent (S10, S11).
4. **Heal prompts in an egg.** Bryant's *caterpillar* captures the prompt verbatim before the work
   and seals the result. Corwin's `he1al` supplies the prompt, with the failing check, its
   reproduction and its plant (S17). The sealed pair is the local prompt archive that D and the SFC
   ask for (S12).
5. **A legend for every coded name.** Bryant's *rosetta* (every project leaves with the legend that
   decodes it), carried by Corwin's *garden-usage*: each shipped tool name (`pv1tl`, `he1al`,
   `pu1sh`) travels with its plain sentence. It answers A's concern about mental load and C's about
   retrieval.

None of the five touches keeper-only material. Whether any is offered to Bryant is Corwin's call.
