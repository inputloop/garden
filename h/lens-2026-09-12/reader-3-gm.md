# reader 3 — the GAME MASTER (verbatim return, 2026-09-12)

1. Dice. "Two d6, different colours; light die reads first." DEAD — "first number higher" does not exist at a table without a coloured die, and neither file says it. Fix: one line at the top of every kit: light die first.
2. d6×d4 index stripes. STUMBLES — `(row−1) mod 4` is a formula, not a pattern. Fix: print the 6×4 grid with six shaded cells; the spoken rule is "shaded = stripe."
3. Rung after a stripe. STUMBLES — only 6 (or 12) of 36 ladder throws land a rung; which shaping table the off-diagonal sends you to is never said. Fix: after a stripe, 1d6 for the rung (1 bottom, 6 top), 1d6 for the shaping table (1–2 good guys, 3–4 callings, 5–6 circumstance). Drop the ladder 6×6.
4. Doubles bump. RUNS as a crit; but "the walk may continue" never says the re-throw continues it, and row 6's neighbour is unnamed. Fix: "bump, throw again, stop on the first non-double; row 6 curves to row 1."
5. Sevens on entry. RUNS — most-common-sum-as-tag is a nice touch; "first throw only" is easy to hold.
6. Level. DEAD — the 2–12 → L1–L6 column is cited and never printed; whether it's the same throw as the cell is ambiguous. Fix: print it (2–3 L1 · 4–5 L2 · 6–7 L3 · 8–9 L4 · 10–11 L5 · 12 L6) and say "same throw, read twice."
7. Ladders as ascent. STUMBLES — samaritan at rung 2 outranks good neighbour at 4; order is by die pattern, interleaving sums; nothing in the book ever moves a rung — a ladder you roll once and never climb is a shelf. Fix: order by weight; give weathering 8–9 "also throw the ladder; keep the higher rung."
8. How many entries. STUMBLES — whether a stripe eats a trait slot or rides on top is unsaid. Fix: "A persona is four index throws; a stripe is a fate on top — throw again for its trait."
9. SIMPLE kit. STUMBLES — three throws per domain, 18 for six domains, against six 11×16 tables; MAIN is ~10 throws. Fix: SIMPLE = one 2d6 throw per trait: cell is the trait, sum is the level, doubles are fate. Same dice, one lookup, no index, no bumps.
10. Hench to Mench. RUNS as one d66 throw, 1/6 and 5/36 exact, fun — but nothing says how a trait picks L1 vs L6, which ladder end "max" means, or what "doubles flip the extreme" flips from. Fix: per trait 1d6: odd L1, even L6; on max, the dark die odd/even picks hench (BAD bottom) or mench (GOOD top).
11. Weathering. STUMBLES — needs three ledgers the sheet doesn't have; three LB traits idle a month is +4 → retire fires 15/36. Which trait drifts is never picked. Fix: three tick-boxes on the sheet, modifiers capped at +3, the d4 picks the trait.
12. System-actor clamp. RUNS — one column, one sentence, the best flavour in the book: the NPC that knows its own shadow. Put the column on the sheet.
13. One page? As written, no. With fixes 3, 6, 9 it fits: dice line, shaded index, level column, four bump lines, two 6-rung lists, three 6-item lists, the clamp.

Missing that every dungeon book has: a worked example (one persona rolled end to end); a character sheet; a first-session walkthrough; a numbered table index (33: 24 trait + 2 index + 1 level + 2 ladder + 3 shaping + 1 weathering); a glossary; edge rules (row 6's neighbour, depth 4's escalation); and the 24 trait tables themselves, which the book points at but does not carry.

First page for a new player: one sheet titled *Roll a Persona* — the dice line, the shaded 6×4 index, the level column, the four bump lines, the two ladders as 1d6 lists — with the worked example on the back.
