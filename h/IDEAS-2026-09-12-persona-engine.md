## MINE (the instrument's; each with a confidence and the falsifier that would drop it)

- **M1 `[~80%]`** — Exact 1/4 entry on a two-d6 index is impossible with a die-symmetric stripe set:
  stripe cells must be 9 of 36; the diagonal cells (doubles) are the only self-symmetric cells and there
  are 6, so 9 = 6 + 3 unpaired off-diagonal cells or 3 doubles + 3 pairs — neither reads at a table.
  Exact 1/4 designs: **d6 × d4** index (24 cells = 6 domains × 4 depths, 6 stripe cells = 1/4, and the
  index needs no numbering: row = domain, column = depth); or 2d6 with a **confirming d4** ("a stripe
  counts unless the d4 shows 1": 1/3 × 3/4 = 1/4); or a d6 × d8 (48-cell) index with 12 stripes. Nearest
  2d6-only approximations: 8/36 (2/9 ≈ .22, doubles-and-sevens minus the two middle pairs) or 10/36
  (5/18 ≈ .28). Falsifier: he names a symmetric 9-cell stripe set I have not seen.
- **M2 `[~75%]`** — The domain × depth index (d6 × d4) is *also* the better "alternate index table" he
  asked for (H4): it makes the grid of grids its own index. The numbered 2d6 index stays as the classic.
- **M3 `[~70%]`** — Two fate ladders replace the one fate table, one per side, six rungs each, ordered by
  the dice that reach them: GOOD (high doubles + high-first sevens) 4-3 bystander-who-helps → 4-4
  samaritan → 5-2 steady ally → 5-5 good neighbour → 6-1 protector → 6-6 mensch; BAD (low doubles +
  low-first sevens) 3-4 grumbler → 3-3 malcontent → 2-5 schemer → 2-2 bad guy → 1-6 saboteur → 1-1
  evil-doer. His poles kept verbatim (samaritan→mensch, malcontent→evil-doer); the middle rungs are mine.
  The "good guys" table (H9: samaritan/volunteer/medic/sage) and the callings axis (arts/industry/
  governance/scholarship/stewardship) are orthogonal to the ladder and become **shaping tables** the
  ladder can bump into. Falsifier: play shows the six rungs don't read as an ascent.
- **M4 `[~65%]`** — Balance is a measurable, not a feeling: over N seeds the counts of good-ladder and
  bad-ladder entries must be equal in expectation (they are, by the die pattern: 6 cells each), *and* the
  intensity distribution reached on each ladder must be symmetric. Print both from the tool for every
  kit; a kit that fails symmetry is "biased" in the news sense and says so.
- **M5 `[~70%]`** — The "curve in track" (H3, low doubles) is best read as *the next row in the same
  table* (a neighbour by synergy), and "same track, greater intensity" (high doubles) as level +1 on the
  same trait. A curve that leaves the table (to the domain's next-deeper table) is the 6-6 escalation
  already in pg1rl and should stay as the rarest move (1/36).
- **M6 `[~60%]`** — Weighted index tables map naturally onto **2d6 sums** (2..12, a triangle) for
  *levels*: a sum-indexed level table makes the moderate middle likeliest — the "balance" the main kit
  wants — while the cell tables keep uniform trait choice. So the main kit can roll trait uniform
  (die = row) and level *weighted* (sum → level via a 2–12 column), with modifiers from prior rolls
  (a load-bearing trait adds +1 to the next sum). This is the dungeon "to-hit table" reused honestly.
- **M7 `[~70%]`** — Hench-to-Mench is a **1d6 gate, then a 1d6 inner gate**: 1 → normal persona (the
  main kit, moderate levels only); 2–6 → extreme kit; inside that, a 1 → *max* extreme (L6 or L1 on every
  trait, ladder top or bottom). That is his "1/6 normal, 1/6 within the 5/6 max extreme" exactly.
- **M8 `[~65%]`** — My expansion: **the weathering deck** — persona *mutation over time* as a weighted
  index table: each session/scene rolls 2d6 + modifiers (mass of untested load-bearing traits, calendar
  time since last movement — context stacking's piles/boulders/mountains) against a table whose rows
  are "no change · drift one level · curve to a neighbour · a new trait affixes at second use · a trait
  retires". It is the mutation engine he keeps asking for ("healthy doses of mutating variation") made
  rules-driven, and it uses his own accumulation vocabulary.
- **M9 `[~60%]`** — For system actors the ladders are *informational*: a bad-ladder entry becomes a
  perspective/watch, never an action licence (see the governance lift in the KITS draft). Mechanically:
  `actor: system` clamps the bad ladder to "what I must watch for in myself" and routes the roll to the
  §3(e) bad-guy stage; the good ladder stays as "what I am for".
- **M10 `[~55%]`** — The skill/MCP question (H8): plan the *contract* now (pg1rl's JSON is the API:
  `--json`, `--roll --json`, seeds, marks), build the server in a separate pass after the kits. A thin
  MCP (three tools: describe · roll · lint) plus a SKILL.md that teaches the table. Planning the contract
  informs the main work (it forces the JSON to be stable); building it now would over-complicate.

## COMMENTS / CONCERNS (tagged; each with its falsifier; [MINE] never blocks)

- **C1 [HIS/DOMAIN]** — H1's line ("stop just short of a directive") vs H1's first sentence ("the engine
  is entitled to have a person"). No conflict if the *entitlement* is a rule in the kit ("a persona may
  affix when…") and the *directive* is what a session does not issue. Falsifier: any kit text that says
  "create persona X". Remedy: the kit says *when* and *how*, never *whom*.
- **C2 [DOMAIN]** — The 1/4 arithmetic (M1). Falsifier: a symmetric 9-of-36 set. Remedy: d6×d4 index as
  the main, 2d6 as the alternate, both printed with their exact rates.
- **C3 [PLATFORM]** — Two RNGs (Python vs the viewer's JS) roll different dice for one seed; both print
  the engine. Falsifier: someone expects parity. Remedy: name the engine in every trace (done).
- **C4 [SKILL: fantasy-operational-boundary]** — "lift the governance, not the sermon": the bad ladders
  are fiction-side; when the persona watches a real system the moral load inverts. Falsifier: a kit
  sentence that licenses a system persona to *act* against principle "in character". Remedy: the
  clamp in M9 as a table row, not a paragraph.
- **C5 [MINE ~50%]** — The near-twin traits across columns (QA fork 2) will show up more often once
  bumps curve into neighbours by design. Falsifier: a 200-seed sweep counting twins per persona. Remedy:
  the "twin = +1 level" house rule, still one line.
- **C6 [MINE ~40%]** — The main book risks being read as a personality test. Falsifier: a stranger's
  first read. Remedy: the marking and the "levels, not scores" paragraph stay at the top of every kit.
- **C7 [PLATFORM]** — Drive hand-carry is expensive and error-prone at 200 KB+; the record TSVs are not
  yet on Drive (INDEX-2026-09-12.md says so). Falsifier: the host carries the .xz in one move.
