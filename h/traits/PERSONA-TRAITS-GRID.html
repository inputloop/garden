Persona Traits Grid

How to play (the short form)

You are at a table with two six-sided dice, this document, and a persona to build — for a story, or for an AI that will help keep some complicated technology running. The first die is always the row, the second is always the column.

  1. Roll on the INDEX. A numbered cell (1–24) sends you to that trait table. The two diagonal

stripes are special: doubles (1-1 … 6-6) is the BIAS stripe — roll the news-bias generator once (you get a source and a fate word: villain · badguy · malcontent · ne'er-do-well) and then roll the index again; sevens (1-6 … 6-1) is the INTERSECTION stripe — roll the index twice more (numbered cells only; a stripe just re-rolls) and take both traits as a pair the persona has to reconcile. A high-first seven (6-1, 5-2, 4-3) is a high-stakes intersection; a low-first seven (1-6, 2-5, 3-4) is low-stakes.

  1. Roll on the trait table you landed in. The row is the trait; the column is the level of

expression, from L1 (faint) to L6 (extreme). The cell holds the words.

  1. Doubles and sevens bump. On a trait table, doubles move you to a neighbouring trait in

the same table: high doubles (4-4, 5-5, 6-6) go to the next row one level up (amp — stakes rise); low doubles (1-1, 2-2, 3-3) go to the previous row one level down (damp). 6-6 escalates: after the bump, roll again on the next-deeper table of the same domain (depth 4 sends you back to the index) — this is the high-intensity intersections idea ("oh, high doubles on the enthusiasm table, roll again and watch out for low doubles"). Sevens stay on the trait but change its weight: high-first (4-3, 5-2, 6-1) → level +1 and the trait is load-bearing (it will be tested); low-first (3-4, 2-5, 1-6) → level −1 and the trait is background.

  1. Repeat for as many traits as the persona needs — 4 for a tiny sketch, 9 for a short

work, 17 for a long one (the house's own numbers). Write each result as a line on the character sheet, keep the dice in the margin, and never re-roll a roll you already have.

  1. Want pure chance instead? Use the JUMBLED twin: the same 36 cells per table, shuffled

by a printed seed, no stripes, no bumps — a totally randomized selection with none of the good-roll / bad-roll affinity above.

With the tool: python3 h/pg1rl --roll PERSONA-TRAITS-GRID.md --seed 20260906 --traits 9 prints the whole trace (every die, every bump, every colour); --html draws the grid of grids on graph paper with a live roller and highlighter. The seed is always printed so a reader can re-roll the same persona.

Highlighter (the table convention): when we view these tables playing dungeon we mark the interesting cell with highlighter on graph paper — pink = scary, blue = chill, green = hip, yellow = worrisome, and sometimes purple = wild. The tool uses the same five: pink for amped bumps, villains and escalations; blue for damped bumps; green for a clean hit in the moderate middle; yellow for sevens and the extreme columns; purple for the index stripes.

Design — why the tables look like this

A grid of grids. Twenty-four trait tables, arranged four across and six down: each of the six rows of the big grid is a domain of human behaviour, and the four tables in that row go progressively deeper into it. Each small table is 6×6 = six traits × six levels, so 24 × 6 = 144 traits, which between them are meant to give a good sense of the range of human behaviour — not a personality test, a wheel or ball of persona in which neighbours share synergy or vocabulary. The neighbors column of the record names each trait's nearest neighbours across the whole ball, which is how you walk from one table to the next by hand.

Where the traits come from. Every trait carries at least three formal sources (a validated instrument, a peer-reviewed study or a meta-analysis — at least one specific to that trait) plus one or two flair sources (a magazine, a popular book, a well-known blog) for contrast. The taxonomies leaned on: the NEO-PI-R and HEXACO facets, the VIA character strengths, Cloninger's TCI, Kashdan's five-dimensional curiosity, the interpersonal circumplex, Zuckerman's sensation seeking, Duckworth's grit, the Dark Triad/Tetrad, HEXACO Honesty-Humility, Schwartz's refined values, the Moral Foundations Questionnaire, Holland's RIASEC, and the standard scales for self-esteem, self-efficacy, self-compassion, locus of control, emotion regulation and mindfulness. Each source was checked by two readers — the PubMed index and a second route (a DOI resolver, a publisher page, or a web search) — and is marked VERIFIED-2, VERIFIED-1 or UNVERIFIED in the record; nothing was typed from memory. The full bibliography is PERSONA-TRAITS-SOURCES.md.

Levels, not scores. The six columns are expressions, written as words a writer can use, from the faintest (L1) to the most extreme (L6). L3–L4 are the moderate middle. These words are judgements (the assembler's and the six research readers'), and they say so in the record's notes; the sources are observations. A level word never claims to be a clinical cut-point, and the dark-traits table describes dispositions, not diagnoses.

The stripes. The index table has 36 cells; the twelve on the two diagonals (doubles, sevens) are stripes, so exactly 24 numbered cells remain — one per table. The stripes carry the two things a flat lookup cannot: the news-bias generator (variance the plausible middle will not produce on its own — the fate dice from LENS persona seeds §3(d)) and intersections (two traits that must be reconciled, which is where a persona stops being a list).

The jumbled twin exists so that the affinity built into the straight tables (rows ordered by synergy, columns by intensity, diagonals as bumps) can be switched off on purpose — the same move as Roll for Bias's randomized matrix: the ordered grid encodes the judgement; the shuffled grid hands you the same items with the ranking stripped.

Method and lineage

This grid is the news/ methods applied to themselves for the first time at depth:

  • Roll for Bias (METHOD-roll-for-bias.md, 1.0.91; the signed 1.0.90 agent artifact) gave the

shape: fold to the extremes and the baseline, order it, then strip the order. The news-bias generator here is its 1.0.90 execution carried forward as it was — 36 sources, ordered and randomized — with one change to fit forward: the fate dice ride with it, so a bias roll also hands the persona a fate. Clarification of 2026-09-06 (his): the ordered table is evenly split between the highly biased (toward the beginning, low indexes) and the moderate (toward the end, the high indexes and the lower right) — one monotone run of |bias| from cell (1,1) to (6,6). The reference implementation h/rf1bm v1.2 sorts that way.

  • LENS persona seeds (METHOD-lens-persona-seeds.md, 1.0.92) gave the fate table and, in

§3(e), the bad-guy roll for system actors: an actor that is more IRL than not takes its one kept roll as a bad-guy perspective and then one more lens stage that inverts it. A persona built from this grid for a system actor still owes that stage.

  • Dungeon modelling (METHOD-dungeon-modeling.md) gave the table itself: characters as

inventories and attributes; the record is TSV, double-entry (every row names its source); a persona rolled here lands on a character sheet with a bench, holdings, gold and specialness.

  • Context stacking gave the sizes — 4, 9, 17 — and the idea that a load-bearing trait gathers

mass the longer it sits untested.

  • Persona affixing (his, 2026-09-06): persona affixes — sometimes, not guaranteed — at a

feature plane, as soon as a feature is used twice; not while building and testing locally. Every character sheet that grows from a roll here opens with a founding journal entry that tells exactly how the persona happened. Two voices in this house (Vibrobeast, the shop's internal voice and curator outward; Mentat, the continuity voice that works memory as a fabric) grew before this engine existed and are kept as pre-engine voices, deliberately, until C4 can reintroduce them in context.

What to revise next (named, not buried): the level words are judgements and should be read against each cited scale's anchor items; four traits are flagged contested in the record (Loyalty, Kindness, Tolerance, Gentleness — value-shaped or thinly scaled); three exact duplicates across columns were resolved by the assembler (Competitiveness → Need for Achievement in C3; Self-Efficacy → Work Ethic in C3; Modesty → Deference in D4) and say so in notes; the parity reading of the fate short-list (even → first option) is the assembler's guess at "even/odd"; the WebSearch budget ran out mid-pass, so the second reader for later columns was the DOI registry rather than a search.
