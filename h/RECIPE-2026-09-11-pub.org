* What this is

The front-door instructions for =<H>:/pub/=. The =.seed= says what the surface *is*; this
file says what you *do* — lift material in (PACK), cut something a stranger can hold
(UNPACK/RELEASE), keep copies current (JANITORIAL), and check before anything travels that
it does not name your machine (LEAK CHECK).

A note on spelling: the surface is written =<H>:/pub/=, the =.seed='s own notation, and
example paths elide the drive letter the same way (=<drive>:\lab\notes.org=). A real drive
letter is what the check hunts for, so the recipe does not write one.

* The surface, in his words

From =.seed=: =/= (the pub root) is the "finished publications stage, instructions"; each
=<item>/= sub-folder is "for publication stage, may be a work surface". Working materials are
"protected via dot folder, gate/tunnel or otherwise safe for general use/reuse" — in practice
the =.c4/= dot-folder inside each item. An item not ready for branding "may so indicate with a
'tombstone' (zero byte) COPYING or by creating that file with whatever informational context
is needed to scope these contexts".

And the rule this whole recipe serves, his: do not leak "details of my systems
unnecessarily with these materials in publication apparatus (nor content proper, obviously,
unless such detail happens to form some material record and so we shrug at leaving whatever
path showing for that reason)".

*Unnecessarily* is the load-bearing word. No tool can tell a leak from a record. The tool
points; you decide.

* PACK — lifting formed material into an item

Formed material is anything already made in the lab that the publication needs: an org or
markdown note, a fact, a deck, a runnable script, a tool.

1. *Copy it in, do not reference it.* The lifted copy lands in =<item>/.c4/sources/=, byte for
   byte. The standing rule, from =motive-inversion/README.org=: "*Self-contained is the rule.*
   … no 'also attach the lab', no 'the file is over in c4'".
2. *Give it a PROVENANCE row.* One line in =<item>/.c4/PROVENANCE.tsv=, tab separated:
   =local_copy=, =lab_origin=, =sha256_16_at_lift=, =lifted=, =note=. Get the hash with
   =sha256sum <file> | cut -c1-16=. The header of that file states why duplication is fine:
   "Duplication is fine because this file tells its story (Corwin's rule); a silent clone is
   the only sin."
3. *=lab_origin= is TREE-relative, always.* Write =c4/h/LENS-….md= or =c4/facts/…=. Never a
   drive letter, home directory, mount point, hostname or user name. The tree path says where
   a thing lives in the work; the absolute path says where your machine keeps it, and only one
   of those is anyone else's business. This file does not travel — but its contents get quoted
   into READMEs, and then they do.
4. *A file authored in the item takes =-= for the hash* and =(authored in this item)= for the
   origin — there was no lift, so there is nothing to compare against.
5. *Re-lift by re-hashing.* Per the PROVENANCE header: "On a re-lift, re-sha the lab origin;
   a changed sha is the observable tear, not a surprise." Update the row; do not quietly
   overwrite the copy and leave the old hash standing.

** What counts as a material record

Sometimes the path *is* the story: a pinned fact's location, a quoted session transcript, an
archived receipt that means nothing without where it sat. His rule from =.seed=: "if
instruments tell the story of the duplicates/mutated clones, then that is story and we keep
it around -at least in journaled out or archived form- in case we want to tell it later."

So a path stays when removing it would destroy the record, and goes when it is only
scaffolding. Two live examples in =motive-inversion/README.org=: the surface-model pointer at
line 48 is scaffolding (it could say "the pub seed"); the model pin at line 84 is arguably
record — it names where the model was fixed. The check reports both and touches neither.
Write the decision into the item's =.c4/= notes so the next release does not re-argue it.

* UNPACK / RELEASE — cutting a distributable

A release is what a comrade holds. Per =.seed=, the pub root carries "the set of latest
distributable paired archive/sig (given I do signing) unless for I share as text with
integrial sig, and then whatever packaging instructions and support mostly perhaps within
README.org".

1. *Run the leak check first.* =bin/pb1ck <item>= — before the archive, before the signature.
   A signature over a leak is a signed leak.
2. *=README.org= is the front door.* Built to be walked up to: what this is, the thesis or
   product in one screen, status, and enough context that a reader need not hold the shop
   vocabulary.
3. *=COPYING= carries branding or a tombstone.* Either the real license and a dated copyright
   line, or the informational tombstone scoping the pre-branding state. Branding is his
   signing motion; key at https://savannah.gnu.org/u/carlc.
4. *=.c4/= never travels* — that is what the dot-folder is for. The exception is explicit: the
   item names travelling paths, one per line, in =.c4/travels=. Nothing else under =.c4/=
   leaves, and the check enforces the same reading.
5. *Cut the pair at the pub root*: the archive and its detached signature, side by side, named
   for the item and the date. Text shared inline gets an integral (clearsigned) signature
   instead.
6. *Where a member might be held back*, prepare the share with =bin/sc3dm= first: it proves per
   member, so dropping one does not break the proof on the rest. =sc3dm= asks whether a member
   may travel; =pb1ck= asks whether what travels says too much.

* JANITORIAL — a currency-pull, not a daemon

The =.seed= gives automation the janitorial side: "C4 or other automation can have the
janatorial side helping to sync common use tools out for these working-group and/or staging
area stages." The shape of that sync is fixed by the PROVENANCE header, quoted whole:

#+begin_quote
SYNC MODEL (currency-pull, not a daemon): at the moment of inception of a mutation, pull a
cloak of currency over the file (bring it current); that pull IS the only local lock.
Reads always win the read-first battles. Write-races are governed by PRIORITY / value-
weighting (C4's, or the personal lens "until on"), by Corwin's deliberation — never a mutex.
There is no fixed source folder: currency is whatever pulled it last at a mutation; drift
stays legible here so a race is a story, never a silent loss.
#+end_quote

Practically: nothing runs on a timer here. Right before you change a lifted copy, pull it
current and re-hash its row. Between mutations, drift may exist and must stay legible.

* LEAK CHECK — what is looked for, and the one line to run

#+begin_src sh
bin/pb1ck <item>
#+end_src

Exit 0 clean, 1 for any leak hit or CHANGED row, 2 for usage. It removes nothing and refuses
nothing; it prints file, line, the pattern that fired, and the matched text with *nothing
either side of it*.

Ten patterns, over every file that would travel (everything outside =.c4/=, plus whatever
=.c4/travels= names):

- *drive-letter* and *unc-path* — a Windows path or a share name.
- *session-path*, *home-path*, *home-var*, *mount-path* — =/sessions/=, =/home/<user>= or
  =/Users/<user>=, =$HOME= and its Windows spellings, =/mnt/=.
- *ip-v4* and *ip-v6ish* — an address that names a box on a network.
- *email-undeclared* — any address that is not on the README's =#+AUTHOR:= or =#+EMAIL:=
  line, or listed in =.c4/mail-ok=.
- *leakword-N* — line N of =.c4/leakwords=, one string per line: your host names, user names,
  machine names, internal project names. Nothing of yours is written into the tool; it knows
  only what the item tells it. A hit reports the line number and *withholds the word*, because
  a report that echoes it has re-leaked it.

It also re-hashes every PROVENANCE row whose =local_copy= exists (MATCH, CHANGED — the
observable tear — MISSING, or NOHASH), warns when a =lab_origin= is not tree-relative, and
counts its findings twice, with grep and then with awk over the emitted report, printing
AGREE or DISAGREE: an uncounted tally is an unchecked one.

The report echoes matches, so the report is local. Do not put it in the item.

* How this recipe can fail (falsifiers)

- *A release goes out with a path that was nobody's business.* Check: run =pb1ck= on the
  released item; any hit you cannot defend as record is this recipe failing.
- *The check passes and the item still leaks.* Check: grep a cut archive for your own hostname
  and user name. A pass with no =.c4/leakwords= file is the likeliest cause — the report prints
  ABSENT in that case, and that line is the warning.
- *A reader cannot re-derive a lifted copy.* Check: take any PROVENANCE row and run
  =sha256sum <local_copy> | cut -c1-16=. If it does not reproduce the recorded value and no
  re-lift was recorded, the instrument stopped telling the story.
- *It fires where it should not.* This is for the pub surface only — lab work and private notes
  are out of scope.
- *Off-switch.* Say "no leak check on this item", or delete =bin/pb1ck=. Nothing here blocks,
  and nothing else is wired to it.
