# LENS 2026-09-02 — PREF for others: the trading-card problem at the fantasy/operational bound

*Corwin's steer: PREF is really tricky because it isn't self-preference — it's holding THIRD-PARTY
preferences and trading them "like cards" (physical cards floated with a person or two; Bryant's
symmetric-encryption; a card carrying a character/universe that also speaks to devices via mesh).
Storing knowledge of others' preferences escalates tension fast. BUT (his council-C4 line):
**we cannot accept intractable entropy of novelty in the name of privacy; even safety must risk some
common ground — to refuse it is a strong political take, not mild, and not one to associate with
safety.** He suspects the problem isn't quite intractable; answers turning up in access, furniture,
signage, and math. Not building ccspt today — tackling the problem head-on. Candidates below; offered,
not asserted solved.*

## The reframe (name the boundary)
The trickiness has a name already in the shop: **fantasy/operational boundary**. A preference card is
a fun object (a character, a universe) AND a real dossier of a person. The mechanism transfers across
that seam; the **moral load inverts** the instant a game piece becomes real semi-personal data traded
in an operational context. That inversion is the "drama." So the design problem is: *keep the fun,
kill the inversion.*

## The stack (several angles; keep all — they compose)
1. **PROJECTION bounds the entropy (edrx = the "math").** Don't store/trade a person; store a
   **projection** — an information-DISCARDING specialization to a concrete, purpose-bound, low-entropy
   face ("prefers concise; MinGW paths"). Provable/usable in the concrete; **liftable** to the full
   picture only by the holder with the reconstruction key. This dissolves "intractable entropy of
   novelty": each card is low-entropy by construction, not the whole person. Novelty can't explode
   because you never hold the person, only a face.
2. **INTUITIONISTIC disclosure (Planar Heyting Algebra).** Privacy is intuitionistic, not classical:
   "not on the card" ≠ "known false." A card carries an OPEN SET of the preference-space; trading =
   sharing opens (monotone); you never assert the complement. J-operators/slashings give a real math
   for *what a card asserts vs leaves undetermined* — the anti-over-claim (credit-bureau vs bank).
3. **INERT-BY-DEFAULT fence (Bryant's symmetric crypto) — the enforcement, not a hope.** The card is
   two layers: a **fantasy face** (freely tradable, zero real load) over a **symmetrically-encrypted
   operational payload** (the projected real preference). Inert — pure fantasy — until a **live,
   consented key** unlocks it in an operational context. Default state is safe; crossing the seam
   REQUIRES a two-key consented act (containment-during-exec's two-key for irreversible reaches). This
   inverts the danger: safe by default, personal only on a consented reach.
4. **SELF-DECLARING SIGNAGE (furniture + the band).** Every card wears its provenance, consent-mark,
   scope, and standing (in-force/superseded) on its face — the tripled signage (colour·shape·word).
   A card that can't show a consent-mark reads UNWIRED (honest absence), never silently assumed
   consented. This is ccspt's receipt→publish gate rendered on the card face.
5. **SYMMETRY defuses the power-tension.** Asymmetric holding (I have your prefs, you don't have mine)
   is where tension/power lives. Reciprocal trading on equal terms (Bryant's *symmetric* crypto is a
   nice literal echo) is the egalitarian posture — the anti-credit-bureau default.

## Direct answer to the doubt: is signage sufficient medicine for the drama?
**No — and that instinct is right.** Signage is the **honesty** layer (you always know what a card
claims and whether it's consented). It is NOT the **enforcement** layer. At the fantasy/operational
seam — exactly where "harmless in the game" inverts to "breach in the real" — a label does not hold a
line; a control does. So: **signage for honesty, crypto + live two-key for the boundary, projection to
bound entropy.** Doubling down on Emacs gives superb *furniture/signage* (everything visible, editable,
provenance-linked — edrx's own ethos) but it is a transparency medium, not an enforcement medium:
necessary, not sufficient, in the same way.

## Principle check (his line, threaded)
This refuses the political over-refusal. Common ground is *risked* (cards exist, are held, are traded)
— safety risks it, as he says it must. Privacy is protected **structurally** (projection + inert-by-
default), not by refusing representation. Entropy is **bounded** (projections, open-sets), not left to
explode. Operational use is **gated** (consent + key), not assumed. That threads "no intractable
entropy of novelty in the name of privacy" without collapsing to store-nothing/share-nothing.

## Where this is weak (the honest eclipse instinct — not yet run)
- Projection LEAKS across cards: enough low-entropy faces reconstruct the person (the mosaic/deanon
  problem). Bound: cap the join, or make lifting the only reconstruction path.
- "Live consent" at the seam can be **manufactured/coerced** (consent-gate's whole reason to exist).
- A physical card is a **bearer token**: possession ≠ consent; loss/theft = silent operational reach.
- Mesh gossip re-introduces asymmetry (who saw the decrypt).
Next honest step: run the eclipse/LENS to try to BREAK the stack on several seeded real cases, keep the
losers. Offered, not done.

## The gatekeeper principle (Corwin, mid-pass) — the honest terminus of "enforcement"
Following "enforcement, not signage" all the way down lands on **physical objects**. A **car key** IS
the enforcement — no policy to trust, no gatekeeper-who-must-behave; possession of the object = the
capability. That is why the card-as-physical-object is the right instinct: it collapses "trust the
policy" into "hold the thing."

But it does not remove trust — it **relocates** it. *"If you have a gate then you have a gatekeeper,
and that gatekeeper is always in somebody's pocket."* The car key relocates the keeper to key-custody
(and the locksmith, the keying, the valet). The gate makes the keeper **concrete and possessable** —
honest, but a keeper nonetheless. Governing rule: **you may try to invent your way OUT of the keeper
(split keys, threshold/quorum, mesh witness, non-extractable hardware) — you may NEVER "just agree it
happened already."** Laundering a keeper into "trustless" by fiat is theatre (signing-model:
comply≈faked only INSIDE a named fence; containment-during-exec: name which boundary you actually
have).

This **reconciles signage and enforcement** — the answer to "is signage sufficient?": *no, and now we
know its real job.* Enforcement is the physical key. **Signage's job is to keep the keeper legible** —
to name who holds the gate and whose pocket it's in — so no one can "agree it happened already." Signage
does not replace the gate; it prevents the laundering. Necessary, sufficient, and load-bearing stay
three jobs: key = load-bearing, signage = necessary (keeper-legibility), neither alone is sufficient.
