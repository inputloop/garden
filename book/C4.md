# C4.md — the generational ambition (Collaborative Consent Continuity Controls)

> Collecting our ambitions for **C4 / ghostwheel** into one brief. Written hard, on
> purpose — Corwin asked me to go hard this round, so the critiques below are not
> softened. Sources: the `c4-2026-03-29` tree, FoaF (`csh/`, `meshpoll-critique/`,
> `FOAF.md`, takushi), and an extreme-debug go-harder torture pass (kept, debugger
> on, ethics released before the persona pass). Status: **phase0 — "works for us,
> tho" — close.** Possibly more than 3–4 of us will run it; that raises the bar.

## 1. What C4 actually is (from its own source)
C4 is a **shell assembler**: *"restate the problem in terms of the solution."* It
organizes program code **and** data as whole and partial **fragment files** on the
filesystems of the hosts where it runs, and integrates integrity checks across
multi-host, multi-user environments using **cryptographically signed phased
mirrors**. The name unpacks to **Collaborative Consent Continuity Controls** — glue
that "caters toward safe and sensibly transparent operational governance by mutual
consent among users."

The idiom (`ghostwheel.fn.sh`): tests-first (run-levels 0–5: bootstrapping →
containers → components → configurations → connections → running), tiny dotted
namespaced functions (`c4.f.dval`), everything env-override-able (`${C4_X-default}`,
"obey existing values"), warn-don't-die, sign/SHA-256 by reflex, heckling comments
and fast-typed typos in the source ("forensenices"). Voice = code.

## 2. The signing door is already real (phase0)
Two pieces already exist and are the spine of everything below:
- **CSH (Challenge-Signature Handshake)** — turn a website into a public key + an
  authenticator. Ed25519/libsodium, one-time nonces, **mutual** auth (server signs
  too), filesystem as datastore, one `authorized_keys` line per identity, revoke =
  delete the line. *"ccspt reduced to one door."*
- **ccspt** — stdlib skill provenance: content digest, in-toto consent statements,
  `ssh-keygen -Y` signing, lenient/strict gates, `init/ingest/verify/publish`. 8/8
  green against a real skill. Per FOAF.md, **the closest thing we have to a product.**

These answer **"who is this, really?"** — the per-identity enrollment ghostwheel
needs before any signed `.skill` or mesh handoff crosses a trust boundary.

## 3. The generational move — compose from signed hunks
Corwin's ambition, stated plainly: C4 grows the ability to **compose programs out of
signed code on the wire**. Like drag-and-drop of an executable — **except inverted**:
not PUSH-to-app, but **pull-and-apply only signed, known, user-included hunks** to
compose. The operator's roster decides which fragments may assemble; nothing runs
that wasn't signed by a key the operator authorized. That is the tool surface for
**trust-boundary, responsibility-bearing components** — like the torture/consent
gate itself. (Torture pass 1 verdict: PUSH-to-app loses to pull-apply-signed-hunks.)

## 4. Folding C4 toward usability *here* (what it takes)
Honest scope, not a wish:
1. **Vendor C4 into the chamber** as an extern (`kind: git`, frozen at a rev) so its
   `memory/` can feed candidates without dragging the whole tree (externs-memory-only
   rule already enforced).
2. **Wire the door into dist.** `polygram dist` should sign bundles via ccspt and
   stamp a CSH identity; skilltank's `consent_gate.py` already refuses an unconsented
   publisher binding — make that gate *call ccspt verify*, not just look for a marker.
   (Today's gate checks a **claim**; it can't prove a consent happened. That's the
   gap to close — §6.)
3. **Fragment granularity.** Define the "hunk": a signed fragment file + its manifest
   line. Reuse C4's filesystem-as-DB 17-letter taxonomy; the consent-quartet
   (identity·package·subscription·register) scopes each hunk.
4. **Enrollment UX.** `ENROLL.md`'s three-step recipe becomes a chamber tool: cut a
   key, hand over the fingerprint out-of-band, add one line. This is the phase0 a
   handful of peers can actually run.

## 5. Hardest-hitting comments from the FoaF chat (unsoftened)
The meshpoll critique and the CSH honesty note are the sharpest things we have. Keep
them load-bearing:
- **"Realtime pub/sub is the commodity, not the moat."** Any WebSocket gives fan-out.
  The differentiator is **durable, seed-hosted, members-only shared state** — survives
  with no server. We over-built the polish and under-built the moat.
- **Late-joiner correctness gap (a real bug we'd have shipped):** poll definitions live
  in a capped ledger and **age out**; a late joiner sees votes for a question they
  never received. Immutable definition belongs in durable KV, not the ring.
- **"Tally in KV is wrong — reject it."** KV is last-write-wins per key; a shared tally
  key means concurrent voters clobber each other. Derive the tally from per-voter
  state. *Use KV for the immutable definition; never the mutable aggregate.*
- **"Anonymous" would be a security lie — it's pseudonymous.** Votes are signed and
  member-visible; you get cross-app unlinkability, not a secret ballot. Claiming more
  than the crypto delivers is the cardinal sin.
- **The agent-secret honesty note (this bears on me directly):** an AI agent **cannot
  durably hold a secret hidden from its operator** — ephemeral workspace, no vault.
  Don't pretend it does. Provision the key where the operator can see it, `chmod 600`,
  and treat operator visibility as **the feature, not a leak.** That honesty *is* the
  consent.

## 6. Honest gaps & risks (the doubt-first pass)
- **The consent gate verifies a marker, not an act.** It is only as honest as the
  claim. Until it calls ccspt/CSH verify, "consented" is *merely data*.
- **Secret hygiene at scale.** `csh/claude_on_CW.secret` exists in a mounted folder
  (correct per the honesty note) — but ">3–4 users" means a roster, rotation, and
  revocation discipline we have not yet industrialized.
- **C4's source is rough** (typos, `~` backup files in-tree, terse to the point of
  opaque). That roughness is *intentional house idiom* (match, don't modernize) — but
  it raises the onboarding cost for the wider group phase0 invites.

## 7. Winners' advice & perspectives (what drove the models that worked)
Reviewing the favorites — meshpoll's blend loop, the chamber harvest model, FOAF.md:
- **Doubt-first beats reward-first.** Counterfactual stress-testing ("strip each
  facet — does it survive?") reaches *which part is load-bearing* faster than
  confirming the hypothesis. Run from **two opposed biases and compare**; convergence
  is the stability signal, not a feeling.
- **Reward/critique → blend, materialized as files.** A model is a stance, a delta is
  a scored signal, a blend regroups. Cheap, auditable, and it caught a real bug.
- **Foundation over progress-on-paper; `should` over `must`; concise over verbose.**
  (Corwin's governance line, FOAF.md §1.)
- **The seam is the leverage.** Corwin governs, Bryant ships, Mentat throughputs — a
  project using all three is worth more than one using one. Don't centralize; federate.
- **Lead with the map + the reap; push detail down.** Keep every model cheap to re-read.

## 8. Load-bearing shapes (from the debugger results)
What the kept torture artifacts say the architecture must rest on:
**durable signed shared state · identity-at-the-door (one key, one line, revocable) ·
filesystem-as-DB · fragment/hunk granularity · run-levels for safe bring-up · the
operator-held roster · atomic verified writes.** Realtime, presence, and polish hang
*off* these — never the reverse.

## 9. 2026-07-09 — the executability layer lands, and a test worth running
The §3 ambition ("compose programs from pull-and-apply signed hunks") and the
§6 gap ("the gate verifies a marker, not an act") both got their answer this
week, minted as models on the pub shelf (`models/c4/`):

- **The Execution Proposal (EP)** — `MODEL-right-to-execute.md`. A hunk's right
  to run is itself a signed claim: **content pin** (sha256 + public repo +
  pinned commit) + **committer pin** (whose key stands behind the bytes) +
  **scope** (registers it may write, run-level ceiling, dominance U/D/C/H) +
  the **user's counter-signature as the grant**, landed in the register as
  PROOF. Sign the *right-to-execute*, not just the identity — the layer the
  industry's Signed Agent Cards stop short of. Assembly-time verification
  (ccspt/CSH verify at compose) closes §6: the gate now checks the *act*, not
  a marker. Alfred's folds ride inside: dumb-assembler / smart-verifier, meta
  inside the signed span, sign-LAST generalized to assembly.
- **The four signing layers** (`c4-0.0.1-ALPHAv0.1.2.md`): identity (CSH /
  Signed Agent Cards) · content (ccspt / skill-sign) · **right-to-execute (EP —
  the open layer, C4's move)** · consent-continuity (the quartet). Anti-lock-in
  baseline named: **nothing in C4 may require an AI agent** — every gate,
  verify, and grant is POSIX-driveable by a human; the agent is convenience on
  a fully free stack.
- **The at-scale red-team = committing science.** The kit was handed to a local
  delegate to demonstrate where these designs weaken *given wide use at scale*
  — keeper-signed bundle, unverified-surrogate output, findings binding once
  keeper-validated, captured-not-published. The honest hypothesis, stated by
  the keeper: minimally this shows a weak delegate "doesn't do much for us,"
  maximally it shows we are *making a safer tool* — either way, hard numbers,
  which is the science. If nothing breaks it's confirmation; if something
  breaks it's the opportunity. (The federation it rides — **ÆTHERNET**, four
  gardens, provisional until seventeen assent — is the "used widely" the review
  models against.)

The through-line C4 has always argued, now with an artifact: *generation is not
standing, and convenience must never outrun provenance.* The EP is where the
"march of little letters" reaches the filesystem without the wire having to
trust the tool that lands them.
