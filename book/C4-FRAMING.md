# C4-FRAMING.md — three propositions, ground against three random projects

> Critical framing first (Corwin's instruction), debugger on. To avoid cherry-
> picking, three projects were pulled at **random** (seed = 2026-06-29) from the
> known list: **savannah-forensics · skzb-dragaera · csh**. Each C4 proposition is
> stress-tested doubt-first against all three — the meshpoll lesson: strip it, does
> it survive? Written hard. Archivist stance: this is a faithful report of what the
> records actually support, not what we wish they did.

## The three propositions under test
- **P1 — common language.** "C4 is *imposed* as a common language across our bridge,
  adding to the STDLIB gateway."
- **P2 — reputable footprint.** "C4 must provide a reputable footprint into the
  Claude-consumer, Free-Software, and sharezies-don't-care spaces."
- **P3 — multi-party consented sandbox.** "C4 is the basis for a new multi-party
  Claude cowork environment where friends build sandboxes and can always *prove* the
  consent under which they run the code they run."

## The grid (does the proposition survive contact?)
| | savannah-forensics | skzb-dragaera | csh |
|---|---|---|---|
| **P1 common lang** | FITS — chain-of-custody = signed phased mirrors; suite already stdlib/POSIX | **FAILS the imposition test** — fiction needs no signed mirrors; forcing the idiom is the anti-pattern | self-referential FIT — CSH *is* the identity gateway |
| **P2 footprint** | STRONG — signed evidence is the whole game | MODERATE — IP attribution (Brust, CC-BY) wants provenance | **STRONGEST** — "turn your website into a public key" *is* the footprint |
| **P3 consented sandbox** | HARD EDGE — adversarial parties never consent; model must not be naive | PARTIAL — multi-author consent is *social*, not cryptographic | FIT but caveated — proves the **key**, not the **intent** |

## The hardest findings (unsoftened)
1. **"Imposed" is the bug.** P1 dies on skzb-dragaera the moment you read it
   literally. C4 as a common language must be **offered, opt-in per a project's
   house idiom** — never imposed. Imposing the infra lexicon on a creative project
   is the same rail-laundering / nailing-to-publisher anti-pattern CORWIN.md §22/§5
   already forbids: each region's idiom is authoritative. Reword the ambition:
   *C4 is offered as a lingua franca; adoption is consented, not assumed.*
2. **Provable consent ≠ provable intent.** P3's "always prove we consented" is only
   as true as the act behind the key. CSH proves *which key signed*; it cannot prove
   a human meant it — and (the agent-secret honesty note) an agent's key is
   operator-provisioned and operator-visible by design. So "prove consent" must mean
   *prove which authorized identity ran this, on an operator-held roster* — an
   honest, smaller, real claim. Don't oversell it (cf. the "anonymous→pseudonymous"
   lesson: claiming more than the crypto delivers is the cardinal sin).
3. **Adversarial parties don't consent.** P3 in the forensics frame: the breached/
   attacker party never opts in. A multi-party consent model that assumes all
   parties consent is unsafe for exactly the work savannah-forensics does. The
   sandbox must distinguish *consenting collaborators* from *subjects of study*.
4. **P2 is the load-bearing pillar.** Across all three random projects, the
   reputable-footprint claim is the one that survives every strip. CSH + ccspt
   already deliver it. Build outward from the footprint; P1 and P3 *depend on it*.

## The reframe the records support
- C4 = a **consented** common language (offered, not imposed) → on top of
- a **reputable signed footprint** (CSH identity + ccspt provenance; the moat) →
  enabling
- a **multi-party sandbox** that proves *which authorized identity ran what*, keeps
  consenting collaborators distinct from subjects of study, and never claims to
  prove intent it cannot.
