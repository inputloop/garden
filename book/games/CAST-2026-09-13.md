# THE CAST — four authors and one editor, rolled not invented

*Opus 5, 2026-09-13. Every persona below was produced by `h/pg1rl --roll` against
`modelthisbook/news/PERSONA-TRAITS-GRID.md`, seed declared before the throw and taken as it landed.
No re-rolls — his rule. The rolls are quoted from the runs in this session's transcript.
Marked: for fiction — no ship dates or licensing or publication dates specifically defined.*

These are fictional authors. They are not pen names for Corwin and they are not pen names for me. What
they are is the Paarfi arrangement: a narrator with opinions of their own, whose opinions the real
people behind the book are content to be argued with by. Each one signs their own book. Each one gets
the last section of it to say something about the arrangement, and they do not all say the same thing.

The grounding is real. Every author's obsession traces to a live 2026 argument in the hobby, researched
this session with sources, and each sheet names what that author reads.

## KIRRA VANTH — *seed 4* — author of **The Commons at the Table**

**The roll.** `index 2+3 → table 6 (B2) 1+6 → Need for Cognition · L5 · seeks out hard puzzles`
(low-first seven, level −1, BACKGROUND) · `index 4+4 → BIAS stripe → news 2+1 → The Wall Street Journal
(least-biased); fate 1+1 → villain` · `table 15 (D3) 3+1 → Loyalty · L1 · switches sides freely` ·
`seven → intersection: table 18 (E2) 3+2 Vulnerable Narcissism · L2 · shrugs off criticism` ×
`table 2 (A2) 2+1 Warmth · L1 · cold, formal` · `table 22 (F2) 3+2 → Benevolence · L2 · helps when
convenient`.

**What the dice gave and what she made of it.** A cold, formal woman who needs a hard problem the way
other people need company, who changes sides without embarrassment, helps when it suits her, and shrugs
off criticism — and whose fate die came up *villain*. She has not contested that. She was, for eleven
years, on the licensing side of a company that owned games, and she wrote the letters. When the ground
shifted under open gaming in 2023 and the ORC licence was built to be irrevocable because one company's
promise had proved not to be, she read the licence, found it sound, and changed sides. She will tell you
that this was not a conversion. It was a re-reading.

**Reads:** the Wall Street Journal, every licence in full, and the comment threads under other people's
licences. Follows the "who owns the rules" argument — the question of whether a game's *system* can be
owned at all, or only its expression — the way some people follow a sport.

**Founding journal entry.** *"I was asked once, in a deposition, to define a game rule. I said it was a
sentence that told you what to do. Opposing counsel asked whether I had ever written a sentence nobody
could own. I said no. That was true then. This book is the other answer."*

**Byline.** `Kirra Vanth` · principal: the reader · owns: nothing she can help owning.

## OSCAR PELL — *seed 9* — author of **Six Sides and a Pencil**

**The roll.** `index 4+5 → table 15 (D3) 3+3 → Need To Belong · L2 · content on own` (low doubles, damped)
· `index 2+2 → BIAS stripe → news 6+1 → Jacobin (biased); fate 3+5 → ne'er-do-well` ·
`table 15 (D3) 1+3 → Attachment Security · L3 · mostly secure, occasional doubt` ·
`index 5+5 → BIAS stripe → news 6+1 → Jacobin (biased); fate 6+4 → ne'er-do-well` ·
`table 8 (B4) 4+6 → Intellectual Humility · L6 · believes nothing firmly, deferential` ·
`table 14 (D2) 2+2 → Trust · L1 · assumes ill intent` (low doubles, damped).

**What the dice gave and what he made of it.** Content alone. Holds no belief firmly and says so. Assumes
ill intent — not of people, of arrangements. The bias stripe came up *Jacobin* twice and the fate die
said *ne'er-do-well* twice, which he considers about right. He plays alone, mostly, at a kitchen table,
and his design ethic is a single sentence he will repeat until you are tired of it: **a game must not
require anything the reader would have to buy.** Not a polyhedral set. Not a subscription. Not colour
vision.

That ethic has a 2026 shape. Print-on-demand costs on the main storefront rose roughly fifty percent in
March 2025 and again in February 2026. A platform can deindex your work in a week under pressure from a
payment processor. Six-sided dice are the cheap, borrowable, large-print, braille-available die — the one
kind you can get in a form a low-vision player can read. He builds on the floor because the floor is the
only part nobody can price.

**Reads:** Jacobin; the DOTS RPG Project's guidance on tagged PDFs and their braille polyhedrals; and
the argument about whether OpenDyslexic actually helps anyone, which he cites as his favourite example of
a kindness nobody checked.

**Founding journal entry.** *"I ran a game for a man who could not see the red pips. I had made the
danger red. He asked me, politely, what he was supposed to do about that, and I did not have an answer,
and I have been writing this book since."*

**Byline.** `Oscar Pell` · principal: the reader · owns: the pencil.

## DELPHINE ROUX-ABARA — *seed 17* — author of **The Flagged Book**

**The roll.** `index 5+4 → table 19 (E3) 3+3 → Fairness · L2 · bends rules for gain` (low doubles, damped)
· `index 3+2 → table 10 (C2) 6+6 → Impulsivity · L6 · reckless, out of control` (high doubles, amped;
6-6 escalates one table deeper) → `table 11 (C3) 5+6 → Autonomy/Self-Determination · L6 · refuses all
direction` · `table 9 (C1) 1+2 → Orderliness · L2 · untidy, improvises` ·
`table 16 (D4) 4+3 → Competitiveness · L4 · likes to win` (high-first seven, level +1, LOAD-BEARING).

**What the dice gave and what she made of it.** Reckless, ungovernable, untidy, competitive to the point
where it is the load-bearing fact about her, and willing to bend a rule if bending it wins. She is the
last person you would expect to love a thousand-page rulebook, and she loves them the way some people
love mountains.

Her thesis is the one thing in this whole set that the research could not find anybody else saying out
loud, which is why it is hers: **the hard part of a big system is not learning it, it is finding the
table again.** Everyone who plays a table-heavy game has solved this privately, with tape flags and
sticky notes and a dog-eared page, and nobody has ever put the solution *in the book*. She has. The
Flagged Book is a game whose index is a playable component.

**Reads:** every errata thread; the GURPS Fourth Edition Revised announcements out of Gamehole Con in
October 2025 (not a fifth edition, point costs unchanged, the two volumes merged); and other people's
marginalia, which she photographs at conventions with permission.

**Founding journal entry.** *"I lost a fight because I could not find the page. Not lost the argument —
lost the fight, in the fiction, my character died, because the table that would have saved her was
eleven pages from where I looked. I went home and put a flag on it. Then I put flags on everything. Then
I understood that the flags were the actual game and the book had been a rough draft of them."*

**Byline.** `Delphine Roux-Abara` · principal: the reader · owns: the mess.

## TEODORO "TEO" ILUNGA — *seed 36* — author of **Make It Out Alive**

**The roll.** `index 3+1 → table 9 (C1) 1+3 → Orderliness · L3 · tidy enough` ·
`index 1+1 → BIAS stripe → news 5+6 → Associated Press (least-biased); fate 2+2 → badguy` ·
`table 12 (C4) 6+4 → Decisiveness · L4 · decides promptly` ·
`table 11 (C3) 3+5 → Work Ethic · L5 · idle time feels wrong` ·
`table 4 (A4) 4+4 → Hope · L5 · tenacious, many pathways` (high doubles, amped).

**What the dice gave and what he made of it.** Tidy enough, decisive, constitutionally unable to sit
still, and hopeful in the technical sense — the psychologists' sense, where hope is not optimism but the
habit of seeing many routes to a goal and expecting to take one of them. His fate die said *badguy*, and
unlike Vanth he *has* contested it, at length, and then kept it, because a man who builds futures for a
living and has never once considered that he might be the antagonist of somebody's story is not someone
you should let build futures.

So his book generates *settings*, not characters — and its central move is borrowed, openly, from the
oldest science-fiction roleplaying game there is: in Traveller, character generation is itself a game,
played in career terms, and in the classic rules you could die before play started. Teo asks the
question nobody asks of that mechanic. **What if the thing with the lifepath is the future itself, and
the future can fail its survival roll?**

**Reads:** the AP wire, because he wants the version with the fewest adjectives; the *Stars Without
Number* faction turn, which he calls the only honest piece of worldbuilding technology; and the Mothership
panic engine, which he admires and has decided not to copy.

**Founding journal entry.** *"My grandmother's village has a date on it. Not a founding date — the other
kind. I have never been able to design a future without asking what its date is. This book is me
finally admitting that out loud and then handing the dice to somebody else."*

**Byline.** `Teodoro Ilunga` · principal: the reader · owns: the date.

## HENRIETTE "HEN" SOLBERG-MAAS — *seed 1774* — **the editor**

**The roll.** `index 3+5 → table 11 (C3) 6+4 → Mastery Orientation · L4 · seeks challenge to grow` ·
`seven → intersection: table 14 (D2) 6+5 Straightforwardness · L5 · blunt, cannot dissemble` ×
`table 20 (E4) 5+5 Shame/Guilt Proneness · L6 · mortified, globally worthless` (high doubles, amped) ·
`seven → intersection: table 8 (B4) 6+5 Self-Insight · L5 · clear-eyed about self` ×
`table 23 (F3) 2+5 Fairness/Reciprocity · L4 · keeps careful score` (low-first seven, BACKGROUND) ·
`table 20 (E4) 5+2 → Regulatory Focus · L3 · balanced approach` (high-first seven, LOAD-BEARING).

**What the dice gave and what she made of it.** Blunt to the point of incapacity for tact, clear-eyed
about exactly how blunt, keeping careful score in the background of every conversation, and — the amped
trait, the one the dice shouted — mortified by her own mistakes to a degree out of all proportion. An
editor who takes a typo in a shipped book personally is an occupational hazard. An editor who takes it
personally *and* can say so plainly, *and* whose load-bearing trait is a balanced regulatory focus —
pursuing the good outcome and preventing the bad one with equal attention — is the one you want in the
room.

**What she brings to the room.** She sits in every review of all four books, and she carries two readers
with her, the two this programme found on 2026-09-12 and has been designing for ever since: **the game
master**, who asks whether a rule survives contact with a table at eleven at night with tired friends;
and **the systems operator**, who asks what happens when the thing you rolled up is wired to something
real and then goes quiet. Her contribution is refusing to let a book serve one and flatter the other.

Her third job is the one the authors resent: she knows what a book costs to print, which storefront takes
which cut, what a community-copies tier does to a launch, and that a 300-page black-and-white hardback
went from about thirteen dollars to about nineteen in one revision of the print-on-demand price list. She
will not let a designer put a full-bleed colour spread in a book whose readers are buying it at cost.

**Founding journal entry.** *"I let a page go with a table on it that did not add up. Two hundred and
forty copies. Nobody died. I still know the page number. I tell every author this on the first day, and
the ones who look uncomfortable are the ones I can work with."*

**Byline.** `edited by Henriette Solberg-Maas`.

## The arrangement, stated once here so the books do not have to

Corwin Brust and an instrument called Mentat made the machine that rolled these five people. The five
of them then wrote four books that disagree with each other. This is not a deception and it is not a
disclaimer either — it is the Paarfi arrangement, in which the narrator has a personality, a grudge, and
a publisher, and the reader is trusted to hold two facts at once.

Each book's back matter takes its own position on this. They were not coordinated, and Solberg-Maas
declined to make them match.
