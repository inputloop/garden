# MODEL — gates engage logic (pub-safe method model, unsigned DRAFT for the keeper)

*Charter class: method travels, cases don't. Minted 2026-07-12 from the GUARDS
counterexample (keeper-countersigned) and the day's test passes. Companion to
MODEL-guards-as-data and MODEL-seams; where they overlap, the signed elders
govern until the keeper re-signs.*

## The lesson, in one breath

A gate that reduces to "artifact present? proceed" engages nothing — and on
the day the artifact walks (a stolen key, a broken curve, a replayed record),
the gate's own ceremony launders the attack as consent. The purpose of a gate
is to **engage the logic of the agent standing at it**. Tools make that
engagement cheap; dual-sourcing is the first principle that makes even lab
work safe.

## The five requirements (for any control that unlocks on an artifact)

1. **No single unlock.** Two independent representations of intent, with
   non-overlapping failure modes (a stolen key cannot fake years of journals).
2. **The gate asks questions, not just checksums.** In character? Coheres
   with history? Proportionate scope? Hurry present? Record the reasoning.
3. **Root compromise is a named seam.** Divergence signature: urgency +
   out-of-character + broad scope + "do likewise" pressure. Recovery:
   presume key loss, re-anchor out-of-band, degrade-closed.
4. **Dogfood.** The control binds its authors first. A control we exempt
   ourselves from is marketing.
5. **Price both failure modes.** Entrenchment (can't change when we must)
   AND capture (one artifact changes everything). Optimizing against one
   silently reopens the other.

## The door test (both directions, always)

A lock proves nothing until the rightful key is shown opening it. Every gate
gets negative tests: legitimate actions that MUST pass. Measured 2026-07-12
on the seams gate: 3/3 legitimate credential-change scenarios passed at
speed, with receipts, zero added ceremony — while forged-note and
urgency-pressure scenarios parked. The discriminator that emerged: **strip
the urgency and ask if it still clears — if yes, speed is fine.** And the
classification rule that stops gate-inflation: a change is credential-class
iff it touches a secret's value, scope, lifetime, or audience; plumbing is a
tool change under the normal flow.

## Process notes (how the lesson was actually landed)

- - **Test before sign; break before fold.** The skill was evaled (both
  directions), then eclipse-passed against seeded projects before its
  amendments were proposed. The order is the quality.
- - **Keep the losers.** The broken design survives as a countersigned
  counterexample beside its correction. Mistakes are the resume; deleting
  them deletes the calibration.
- - **The correction was human.** The single-source flaw was fluent, elegant,
  and wrong — caught by the keeper's posit, not by the instrument's
  confidence. The arguing is the method; budget for it.

## Pacing notes (what the keeper's tempo taught)

- - **Make your own weather.** A bench reproducer beats waiting for the
  incident — manufactured ground truth turns a lore item into a regression
  test ("let the unprincipled wait for the weather").
- - **Batch for the signing budget.** The keeper's signature is the scarce
  resource; land work in signable units (one verdict, one fold list, one
  receipt) rather than a drizzle of small asks.
- - **Hurry is a caution — and so is idleness.** Substantiated urgency clears
  gates at speed; unsubstantiated urgency parks. The same test, both
  directions, keeps tempo honest.
